# Brandbook and end-of-term deck (The Level Beam)

Scope: brand guidelines boards with website and app examples, plus the final presentation deck and report that use the same system. Mode: Persuade (deck, website) / Operate (auction desk, app).
Audience: general public bidding on a phone or laptop, often in the last minutes; for the deck, an instructor reading a senior project.
Job: trust the price and the clock, bid without help; for the deck, show the MVP, the method and the brand in about six minutes.
Memorable moment: the beam settles level once, and a 1808 print of an auction hall opens the deck.

## Direction contract
THESIS: Fairness is balance. The mark is two identical weights on a level beam, and everything else behaves the same way: one clock, one winner, one price. Refuses the earlier timetable-yellow direction, the beige and terracotta palette, hard rules and hype.
OWN-WORLD: Cool paper (#F4F6F3) and ink (#0F1512), sage moss (#3B5F4F) for action and dark fields, one brass accent (#85601C, #DDBB70 on dark). Bricolage Grotesque Light for headlines with one brass word, Instrument Sans for reading, Outfit Medium for the wordmark only, tabular figures. Museum public-domain photography of scales and auction rooms leads.
STORY: The visitor sees real objects of weighing and bidding, understands the system keeps every bid level, and bids from a desk that shows price, written closing time and one commitment line.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Deck: full-bleed print of Christie's Auction Room (Met, CC0) under the headline "An auction where every bid weighs the same." Brandbook: moss field, mark, same headline.
SIGNATURE: The one raised object is the bidding desk. In the deck, slides arrive as a level edge rising, then settling.
HONEST RISK: Mixed light and dark grounds, and museum images that are not Adal's own; sellers' photos replace them in a real product.
